# Competitive position

Store readiness row 1.d asked for this before the word "only" appears anywhere
public. Swept 2026-08-26. Every figure below is a third-party estimate with its
source named — none of it is measured by us, and none of it should be quoted as
fact in a listing.

## The finding that matters

**The letter wheel is not ours and is not novel.** Wordscapes is built on
exactly that mechanic — a wheel of letters you swipe to spell words that fill a
grid — and it is the revenue leader of the entire category. Any copy that
implies we invented, own, or are alone in the wheel is false and would be
trivially disproved by anyone who has seen a word-game ad.

This is worth stating plainly because the internal shorthand for this product
is "the dial", and it is a short step from there to writing "the only game
where you spell on a dial." That sentence must not ship.

## The field

| Game | Mechanic | Why it is here |
|---|---|---|
| **Wordscapes** (PeopleFun / AppLovin) | Letter wheel → crossword grid | Structurally the closest thing to us. Category revenue leader. |
| **CodyCross** (Fanatee) | Crossword with **themed, clued** puzzles | The closest thing to our *content* claim, not our mechanic. |
| **Words of Wonders** | Letter wheel → grid, landmark themes | Same wheel, plus a light theme wrapper. |
| **Word Cookies** | Letter wheel → word list | Same wheel, no grid. |
| **NYT Spelling Bee** | Seven letters, one centre, open-ended hunt | The daily-ritual comparison, not the mechanical one. |
| **NYT Wordle / Connections** | Daily guessing / grouping | Sets the daily-habit expectation players arrive with. |

Wordle-alternative roundups are dense and interchangeable — Quordle, Octordle,
Crosswordle, Contexto and a long tail. None of them is a wheel game. They
matter for **habit expectations**, not for mechanics.

## Scale, so nobody plans against a fantasy

- Word games are roughly a **$4.2B** market as of 2024, with app revenue
  projected near **$3.36B**. [Sources: WordsRated, Crosswordle state-of-play]
- Wordscapes is estimated around **$226,000 per day**, of which roughly **55%
  is advertising** rather than IAP, on about **300k downloads and $2M in a
  month**. [Source: Gamigion / Sensor Tower commentary]

The ad share is the number with a consequence. The category leader monetizes
mainly through ads, and this product ships with **zero analytics or ad SDKs and
`connect-src 'self'`** — which is a deliberate, documented position and also
means the dominant business model in the category is closed to us without
reversing a privacy claim already filed as "Data Not Collected". That is a real
tradeoff, not an oversight, and it belongs in the monetization ruling.

## What is actually differentiated

Not the wheel. Three things, in descending order of defensibility:

1. **The rows are one anagram family.** Every answer comes from the same six
   letters, and the board is those six letters exhausted. Wordscapes gives you
   a wheel per level; the constraint here is that a base is only ever six
   letters, which is also why `mantle` and `mental` are the same puzzle.
2. **Cited, themed clues.** 300 hand-authored puzzles across 15 themes, most
   describing Black American cultural life, where a clue is a factual claim
   carrying a canon citation and the build fails when a cited clue is edited
   without its source. CodyCross is themed and clued, so this is a difference
   of *kind and rigour*, not of existence — and the honest phrasing is about
   what the clues are made of, never "the only themed word game."
3. **Finite and daily**, not an infinite level grind.

## What this means for the listing

- Never write "only", "first", or "unique" about the wheel.
- Do not compete on wheel keywords. Wordscapes owns that search surface and
  outspends everyone in it.
- Lead on theme, clue and culture, which is where the actual work is and where
  the field is thin.
- Claim 2 above is the one that needs the cultural reader (store row 1.10)
  before it is made in public. Describing the content as researched is a claim
  about rigour, and the rigour is not complete until a real reader has seen it.

## Limits of this sweep

Web search over roundups and analytics commentary. It is **not** a systematic
App Store and Play listing crawl, and it did not review changelogs. It is good
enough to stop a false "only" claim and to aim the ASO work. It is not good
enough to assert that no dedicated six-letter-base themed wheel game exists —
that is an unproven negative, and it should stay unasserted.
