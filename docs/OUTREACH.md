## The rules that bind every line below

These are not tone preferences. Three of them are board rulings and one is a
recorded operator instruction.

1. **Never *only*, *first* or *unique* about the dial.** Wordscapes is built on
   the letter wheel and leads the category — `docs/COMPETITIVE.md`. The internal
   shorthand for this product is "the dial", which puts that sentence one slip
   away at all times. What is unusual here is the clue writing, not the input.
2. **The packs are made for the people in them first.** Never a lesson, a tour,
   an introduction or a teaching moment for anybody else. Inverting this is the
   single most damaging thing that can be written about this product, and it is
   the easiest mistake to make in good faith.
3. **No numbers we do not have.** No players, no downloads, no ratings, no
   retention. There is no telemetry and no released build; there is nothing to
   round up. A pitch that leads with traction has none to lead with, so it leads
   with the work instead.
4. **Ten sends, then stop and look.** The portfolio's ten-send discipline —
   `docs/STORE_READINESS.md`, phase 4. Ten, individually written, then read what
   came back before the eleventh. Spraying the list is how a list stops being
   worth anything.

## The three pitches

Copy-ready. Each is one paragraph because one paragraph is what gets read.
Every claim in them is in `docs/LISTING.md`'s ledger with a check behind it.

### Pitch A — communities (tier 1)

*Peer to peer, first person, no pitch language, no links beyond the game. Post
it, do not "reach out" with it.*

> I built a daily six-letter word game. Six letters sit on a dial, six rows to
> fill, and every answer is spelled from those same six letters — the board is
> the six letters exhausted. What I actually spent the time on is the clues:
> every row has a line written by hand rather than a dictionary definition.
> It is free, it runs in the browser, there is no account and it collects
> nothing. sixonthedial.com — I would genuinely like to know where it loses you.

### Pitch B — category press (tier 2, needs a listing)

*The angle is the constraint and the citation discipline, not the wheel.*

> Six on the Dial is a daily word game built on a constraint most of the
> category does not use: every answer on a board comes from one six-letter
> anagram family, so the board is a single set of letters worked out to its end
> rather than a wheel per level. The clues are written by hand and the themed
> packs are researched — the research is cited in the public repository the
> game is built from, and the test suite fails the build if a cited clue is
> edited without updating its citation. It ships with no analytics, no
> advertising and no account; the shipped content-security policy allows it to
> talk to its own origin and nothing else. Press kit and fact sheet at
> sixonthedial.com/press.

### Pitch C — culture desks (tier 3, blocked on readers)

*Blocked until `s5i` clears. Written now so that when it is sendable it is not
written in a hurry.*

> Most of the themed packs in Six on the Dial describe Black American cultural
> life — the cookout, the barbershop, Sunday dinner, 90s R&B, spades, the HBCU
> yard. They are written to be recognised by the people they are about; they are
> not an introduction to anything for anybody else. Each clue is a factual claim
> with a citation behind it, and the citations are public. Before any pack was
> sold, a paid reader from the community it describes read it and could reject
> it. [**This sentence is the whole pitch, and it is not true yet — do not send
> this pitch until it is.**] Press kit at sixonthedial.com/press.

## Routes: why there are no addresses in this file

A contact address written down today and used in three months is a bounce, or
worse, a live address for someone who left the beat. Every route below is named
by its **kind** — the thing that stays stable — and the actual address is looked
up at the moment of sending.

- **Community** — post it yourself, in the venue, under your own name.
- **Tips line** — the outlet's public tips or submissions page.
- **Named writer** — find who has covered this category in the last 90 days and
  write to that person. Never a generic newsroom address for a story this small.

**Fit judgments below are reasoned, not researched.** They come from reading the
category, not from a media database, and none of these outlets has been
contacted. Confirm the outlet still covers the beat before you write to it.
Re-check the whole table if it has not been used by **2026-12-09**.

## What not to do

- **No embargo, no launch day, no exclusive.** The web game has been public
  since August. Manufacturing a moment invites the question of what changed, and
  the honest answer — "it is now on Play" — is not a story on its own.
- **No press release.** Twenty individually-written paragraphs beat one release
  sent to twenty inboxes, and this list is not twenty long on purpose.
- **Never send tier 3 to make up for tier 2 not answering.** The culture desks
  are gated on readers for a reason that has nothing to do with how the rest of
  the list is going.
- **No screenshot that is not from the shipped build.** Everything in
  `/press` is captured by script from the real thing, and the capture refuses to
  finish if a still is not the look it claims to be.

## What to measure, since there is nothing else to measure with

No analytics ship and nothing is attributable — `docs/OBSERVING.md` says so
plainly about installs, and it is just as true here. So outreach is measured the
only way it can be: **replies, and reviews.** The weekly install and rating
numbers live in OBSERVING's log; the sends live below, because a send is not a
weekly number and mixing them would lose both.
