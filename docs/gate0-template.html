Six on the Dial Gate Zero

The cheapest test that can kill this game, run on strangers, with the kill rule written down before the first person touches it. It asks one question: can somebody who has never seen this work out what to do, without being told?

Predicted

The player board already says this fails

Seat 1 of the player bench — a first-ever word-game player — is recorded as gone at 0:40, because the board never says what the goal is in words. She does not know she is meant to spell a six-letter word, or that the short rows are the same letters. That is a persona, not a person, and a persona cannot rage-quit. This gate exists to find out whether the prediction is real.

How to run it

One stranger, one variant, first exposure only. A person who has seen any variant is spent for the rest — order effects otherwise make “best variant” meaningless.

  1. Hand them the phone with the app already open. Say nothing beyond “have a go at this.” Do not name the game, the goal, or the wheel. The whole test is what the screen communicates on its own.
  2. Start a timer and do not speak for 90 seconds. Not to encourage, not to correct, not to laugh. Every hint you give is a hint the App Store cannot give.
  3. Note the moment they land a first valid word, or the moment they stop trying. Both are the data.
  4. Then ask exactly two questions. “What is this game asking you to do?” then “What are the boxes at the top?” Anything you add contaminates the answer.
  5. Score it once, on this page, on the variant they saw. Note a bug-read — “is it broken?”, “did that not work?” — in the Miss column, because a game read as broken is a different failure from a game read as hard.

Scoring

ScoreWhat it means
ReadLands a valid word unaided inside 90 seconds and states the rule — words come from the six letters, the boxes are the words to find. Both halves, not either.
PartialOne half only. Spells something but cannot say what the boxes are, or explains the rule but never lands a word.
MissNeither, inside 90 seconds. Record a bug-read separately.
QuitHands it back or stops trying before 90 seconds. Record the timestamp — when is the finding, not that.

The kill rule

Written before any stranger sees this, and frozen once the first person is tested. A threshold that moves after the data arrives is not a threshold.

Frozen

60% Read, no more than 20% Quit, minimum 12 strangers across the set

Sixty rather than Snug's seventy because that test showed a two-second clip and this one hands over a live game with a written teach card — a lower bar would be flattering, and a higher one would fail a game that is merely unfamiliar rather than illegible. Below 40% Read on every variant, the onboarding is not the problem and the mechanic is, and no amount of copy fixes that. Between 40 and 60, the winning variant ships and the gate re-runs.

This is a kill screen, not a validator. It can prove the first run illegible. It cannot prove anyone will come back tomorrow, and retention is the thing this product actually lives on. Passing here earns a day-2 test, not a launch.

Before you test anybody

Gate zero is not runnable today — three of the four variants do not exist, and one item below will silently invalidate every run after the first if it is skipped. Ticks save into the page.

    The four variants

    Same game, four first-runs. D is the control and exists so the other three have something to beat — without it, any number looks like a result.

    What this cannot tell you

    Twelve strangers is enough to detect “nobody understands this” and nothing finer. It cannot rank two variants that land within about fifteen points of each other, it cannot measure whether anyone returns, and it cannot speak to the themed packs at all — a stranger in a hallway is not the audience those were written for, and testing cultural content this way would be worse than not testing it. That is store row 1.10, and it needs a real reader per pack, not a stopwatch.
